\begin{abstract}
The Boltzmann distribution associated with the tied-key/value modern Hopfield
energy is an explicit finite Gaussian mixture: its component means are the
stored memories, its isotropic component variance is the inverse temperature,
and its mixture weights are determined by memory norms and optional
multiplicities. Consequently, independent equilibrium samples require only a
categorical draw followed by a Gaussian draw; Langevin dynamics is not needed
for this base endpoint-sampling problem. We derive the exact density, identify
softmax attention weights as posterior component responsibilities, and show
that the familiar attention-minus-state expression is the exact mixture score.
This interpretation also clarifies that hard memory masks and multiplicity
biases change mixture support or weights without destroying exact sampling. We
then reanalyze the experiments previously used to motivate stochastic
attention. On MNIST, all-component exact samples reproduce the reported
finite-step Langevin novelty, diversity, and energy statistics at both studied
temperatures. On a small protein family, exact samples reproduce the same
qualitative composition and pairwise-statistic behavior after nonlinear
inverse-PCA decoding; a matched local-neighbor perturbation performs similarly.
These diagnostics characterize a memory-centered local model, not a general
open-ended generator, and novelty or diversity alone do not establish sample
quality. We retain ULA and MALA as optional finite-time dynamics and validate
them against the exact law on a tractable target. The resulting separation
between probability model and sampling algorithm gives a simpler and more
accurate account of what modern Hopfield energy provides in low-data settings.
\end{abstract}
