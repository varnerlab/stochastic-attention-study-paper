\begin{abstract}
Attention retrieves a softmax-weighted average from stored values. We develop
stochastic attention, which applies Langevin dynamics to the modern Hopfield
energy so that the same attention operation supplies an analytic sampling
drift. Each update combines state contraction, a softmax pull toward stored
memories, and thermally calibrated Gaussian noise. The construction requires no
learned score network and extends to proper targets that add a weighted,
differentiable observation, penalty, likelihood, or design energy to the
Hopfield energy. We also
derive an exact special case: the unmodified tied-key/value Hopfield Boltzmann
law is an isotropic Gaussian mixture centered on the memories. Softmax weights
are its posterior component responsibilities, attention minus state is its
score, and ancestral sampling gives independent base-target draws. Thus exact
sampling and continuous-time Langevin dynamics implement the same base law,
while finite-step unadjusted Langevin dynamics approximates it and applies to
more general constrained energies. We give the complete sampling algorithms,
regularity analysis, responsibility-entropy criterion, masking construction,
finite-time trajectories, and multi-domain experiments. Exact draws were close
to the reported Langevin endpoint summaries on MNIST and a small protein
family, showing which observations belonged to the target and decoder rather
than to one integrator. The protein analysis supported in-sample family
statistics but not folding, function, or held-out generalization. The result is
a calibrated theory of stochastic attention in which an analytically tractable
mixture is the base case and gradient-based sampling is the general
construction.
\end{abstract}
