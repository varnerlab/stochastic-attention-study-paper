\begin{abstract}
We introduce stochastic attention as Langevin sampling driven by the modern
Hopfield energy: each update combines a softmax attention operation, a
contractive state term, and thermally calibrated Gaussian noise. Because the
construction requires only energy gradients, it extends directly to targets of
the form $\exp\{-\beta[E(\boldsymbol\xi)+\lambda C(\boldsymbol\xi)]\}$, where
$C$ represents a differentiable constraint, likelihood, or design objective.
For the unmodified tied-key/value Hopfield energy, we additionally prove that
the Boltzmann law is an explicit finite isotropic Gaussian mixture centered on
the stored memories. In this special case, softmax attention weights are
posterior component responsibilities, attention minus state is the exact score,
and an ancestral Gaussian-mixture sampler provides independent reference
draws. Thus Langevin stochastic attention and the exact sampler describe the
same base target, while the Langevin construction applies beyond the special
case in which the mixture representation survives. We restore the full ULA and
MALA algorithms, regularity and convergence results, temperature analysis,
masking, multi-head formulation, and finite-time trajectory experiments. Exact
draws reproduce the reported endpoint statistics on MNIST and a small protein
family and expose warm-start and discretization effects. These results sharpen
the empirical claims without demoting the sampling method: the Gaussian
mixture is an exact calibration case, whereas stochastic attention is the
general energy-based mechanism used by the downstream applications.
\end{abstract}
