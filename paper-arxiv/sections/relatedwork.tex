Modern Hopfield networks connect dense associative memory with dot-product
attention~\cite{ramsauerHopfieldNetworksAll2021,krotovDenseAssociativeMemory2016}.
Work on their capacity and temperature-dependent behavior studies retrieval,
condensation, and random-pattern limits
\cite{lucibelloExponentialCapacityDense2024,koulischerExploringTemperatureDependent2023}.
Our result concerns a different but elementary property of the same continuous
energy: its normalized Boltzmann law is a finite Gaussian mixture. The result
does not imply that a general transformer head defines this density, because the
derivation requires tied keys and values in a common state space.

The probabilistic interpretation is closely related to kernel views of
attention. Tsai et al.~\cite{tsaiTransformerKernel2019} formulate attention as a
kernel smoother, while Nguyen et al.~\cite{nguyenProbabilisticKeys2022} model
attention keys using Gaussian mixtures. In the equal-norm case studied here,
the exact Hopfield law is also an isotropic Parzen estimator whose bandwidth is
$h=\beta^{-1/2}$~\cite{parzenEstimationProbability1962}. Thus KDE sampling is
not an external competitor at the matched bandwidth; it is the same probability
model. Nearest-neighbor interpolation and perturbation are distinct local-memory
baselines and are included in our reanalysis.

ULA and MALA are standard tools for targets known through an energy or
score~\cite{robertsTweedieExponentialConvergence1996,durmusNonasymptoticAnalysisUnadjusted2017}.
Stochastic attention specializes these methods to attention-derived energies.
The exact mixture form of the unmodified tied Hopfield target provides an
unusual analytic reference against which its Langevin implementations can be
checked. Once the energy is composed with a generic constraint or likelihood,
however, that independent sampler generally disappears while the gradient-based
construction remains. This is the usual situation for learned energy and score
models, whose density or normalizer is unavailable
\cite{lecunTutorialEnergyBased2006,songHowTrainEnergyBased2021,songScoreBasedGenerativeModeling2021}.

Low-data generation also requires comparisons beyond a VAE with a fixed unit
Gaussian prior~\cite{kingmaWellingAutoEncoding2014}. Geometry-aware VAEs model
the latent support explicitly in high-dimensional, low-sample settings
\cite{chadebecGeometryVAE2023}; iterative encode--decode maps expose attractors
and memorization in trained autoencoders~\cite{fumeroLatentDynamics2026}. These
are appropriate baselines for a complete low-data study. We distinguish them
from the exact correction established here and do not claim that the submitted
VAE comparison settles relative generative quality.
