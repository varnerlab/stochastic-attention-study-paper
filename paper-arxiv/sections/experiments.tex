\subsection{Evaluation scope}

We reanalyze the submitted experiments to answer two narrower questions: whether
the implementation agrees with the exact target, and what behavior belongs to
the memory-centered distribution rather than to Langevin dynamics. Novelty is
one minus cosine similarity to the nearest memory, diversity is mean pairwise
cosine distance, and energy is the model's own Hopfield energy. These are
descriptive geometric diagnostics. Noise can increase novelty and diversity,
and Hopfield energy is not a neutral cross-model quality metric; none is used
here as a stand-alone measure of realism or method superiority.

All-component exact sampling means that the categorical draw ranges over every
stored memory. We also examined an initialization-matched diagnostic with five
draws from each of the 30 memories used to initialize the submitted chains.
That diagnostic is not the stationary all-component law and is reported only
to identify finite-time warm-start effects. Code tests additionally verify the
analytic score by finite differences and recover the nonuniform weights induced
by unequal memory norms.

\subsection{Exact target validation}

We first constructed a two-dimensional, three-component target with deliberately
unequal memory norms ($\beta=1.5$). Its component weights, mean, and covariance
are analytic. We compared 100,000 exact draws with pooled ULA and MALA samples
from 16 chains of 100,000 steps, discarding 20,000 steps and thinning by 20
(Table~\ref{tab:exact-validation}). Exact independent draws recover the moments
up to Monte Carlo error. MALA remains close across the tested steps, while ULA
shows visible covariance bias at $\alpha=0.2$. At $\alpha=0.01$, MALA accepts
every proposal but has larger covariance error than at $\alpha=0.05$ or $0.2$
because its small moves mix slowly. This directly demonstrates why acceptance
rate alone is not an accuracy or convergence diagnostic.

images ($d=784$) and the two reported temperatures. Table~\ref{tab:mnist-exact}
compares the submitted 30-chain ULA endpoints with exact draws from all 100
components. Exact uncertainty is the standard deviation across 20 independent
sets of 150 endpoints; the submitted ULA rows are the original point estimates
and are not assigned comparable repeated-run uncertainty.

The all-component exact law reproduces the submitted ULA statistics within the
variation observed across exact endpoint sets. The matched Parzen estimator is
identical by Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-mixture}, and the previously reported
Gaussian-perturbation baseline was not matched: it used the one-step innovation
scale $\sqrt{2\alpha/\beta}$, which is $7.07$ times narrower than the target
component scale $\beta^{-1/2}$ at $\alpha=0.01$. A $k$NN interpolation followed
by matched Gaussian noise also gives similar novelty, diversity, and energy.
These results attribute the endpoints to local perturbation around memories,
not to additional expressiveness created by a Langevin trajectory.

For a draw generated from unit memory $\mathbf m_k$, the approximation in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:novelty-approximation} gives novelty 0.152 at $\beta=2000$ and
0.549 at $\beta=200$, close to the observed 0.152 and 0.548. The agreement
explains why novelty increases with component width; it does not establish that
the wider, visibly noisy samples are higher quality. We therefore remove the
previous comparisons claiming victory over a VAE or DDPM from these metrics.

\subsection{Protein endpoint reanalysis}

The protein experiment uses the same 68 aligned PF00076 sequences, one-hot
encoding, 59-dimensional PCA representation, inverse-PCA reconstruction, and
per-position argmax decoder as the submitted study. Table~\ref{tab:protein-exact}
reports point estimates from a fresh exact-sampling run. Pos-KL compares
per-position amino-acid frequencies, and MI $r$ is the correlation between
pairwise mutual-information matrices. Both references are computed from the
same 68 sequences used to fit PCA and construct the memories; they are
energy-independent but in-sample. HMM pass rate is also nondiscriminating here,
because every method passes.

Exact sampling reproduces the qualitative temperature dependence and the
headline in-sample statistics without a Langevin chain; the local $k$NN
baseline performs similarly. At $\beta=8$, perturbations are large relative to
a unit-norm memory. Inverse PCA restores the empirical family mean, and the
per-position argmax decoder therefore favors consensus residues at conserved
positions while remaining sensitive to perturbations at variable positions.
The improved composition statistics at wider component variance belong to this
mixture-plus-decoder pipeline. They are not evidence that a Langevin trajectory
learns co-evolutionary constraints. A held-out sequence split or external
functional measurements are required for a generalization claim.

\subsection{Reinterpretation of masking}

The submitted Olivetti experiment reported 96.0\% subject recovery after
masking all memories outside the requested subject, compared with 10.4\%
without the mask. Equation~\eqref{eq:exact-mixture} makes the mechanism
explicit: setting off-subject multiplicities to zero restricts the categorical
support to that subject. The result is a useful memory-subset operation but not
evidence for a general classifier-free conditional generator, and the same
masked target can be sampled exactly without a trajectory.
