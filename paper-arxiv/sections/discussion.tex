The central conclusion is a separation of model and algorithm. The tied
modern-Hopfield energy defines a simple, normalized, memory-centered Gaussian
mixture. Attention computes its component responsibilities, and the exact score
is available analytically. ULA and MALA can explore this law, but neither is
needed to produce independent equilibrium endpoints. Exact ancestral sampling
is faster, unbiased, and provides the appropriate reference against which
finite-step dynamics should be evaluated.

The reanalysis also narrows the empirical interpretation. Exact all-component
draws reproduce the submitted MNIST endpoint statistics and the qualitative
protein results. A bandwidth-matched KDE is the same model, and a local $k$NN
perturbation is competitive on the reported diagnostics. These findings do not
make the memory-centered distribution valueless: simple local distributions can
be useful in small-data applications. They do show that the value belongs to
the distribution, representation, decoder, and application-specific
validation—not uniquely to Langevin dynamics.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The model has support around observed memories and should be described as local
memory-supported generation rather than open-ended generation. At low
$\beta$, the component variance grows and novelty or diversity can increase
because samples become noisy. The MNIST experiments lack an external realism
metric, and the protein statistics are computed against the same family used
to construct the representation. The submitted VAE used a fixed latent prior;
geometry-aware latent-density and iterative encode--decode baselines remain
necessary for a complete learned-model comparison. The former DDPM comparison
used an inadequately justified scale and compute protocol and is not retained
as evidence of superiority. Hard masking and multiplicity remain exactly
sampleable, and the tied-key/value derivation does not establish an integration
with arbitrary pretrained multi-head attention.

Finite-step ULA has discretization bias, while MALA can mix slowly even when its
acceptance is near one. Warm-started multi-chain endpoints may reflect the
chosen initialization allocation rather than stationary component weights.
The all-component exact law must therefore be reported first. Sequential use of
a Markov chain also costs one or more $\mathcal O(dK)$ attention operations per
step, whereas independent exact endpoints require only a precomputed
categorical distribution and Gaussian noise.

The natural next methodological question is not how to resample the same base
mixture with a more elaborate chain. It is how to sample a target in which the
memory prior is combined with an external constraint or likelihood. A general
tilt $p_0(\boldsymbol\xi)\exp[-C(\boldsymbol\xi)]$, hard feasible set, or product
of memory experts need not retain cheap ancestral sampling. In that setting,
exact draws from $p_0$ can serve as global proposals while MALA, constrained
Monte Carlo, or annealed methods handle the additional factor. Such constrained
targets are future work and are not evidence for the claims evaluated here.
