The central conclusion is that stochastic attention and the exact Gaussian
mixture occupy different levels of generality. Stochastic attention is the
energy-based sampling construction: it applies whenever the target energy and
its gradient can be evaluated. The GMM is an exact identity for the unmodified
tied-key/value Hopfield target. In that special case, continuous Langevin
dynamics, MALA, and exact ancestral sampling share the same target, while
finite-step ULA approximates it. This equivalence gives stochastic attention a
rare analytic test bed; it does not invalidate or supersede the sampling
formulation.

The constrained target $E+\lambda C$ makes the distinction operational. A
differentiable $C$ can express observations, mechanistic penalties, fitness
criteria, or products of experts without retraining the memory model. Such a
tilt generally couples or reshapes the Gaussian components and no longer admits
the base ancestral sampler, but the stochastic-attention and MALA updates only
require adding $\lambda\nabla C$ to the drift. This provides the direct bridge
from the present theory to application papers that use stochastic attention as
a constrained sampler.

The exact-mixture result also improves the empirical analysis. Exact
all-component draws reproduce the submitted MNIST endpoint statistics and the
qualitative protein results, showing that those equilibrium-level effects are
properties of the memory energy and decoder rather than artifacts of one
numerical integrator. Conversely, discrepancies among exact draws, warm-started
ULA, and MALA reveal allocation, mixing, and discretization effects. The two
sampling routes are therefore useful together: exact draws establish the base
law, while Langevin trajectories expose finite-time behavior and extend to
targets for which no exact route exists.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The exact GMM theorem assumes tied keys and values in a common state space, the
quadratic Hopfield state term, and no generic external tilt. It does not imply
that an arbitrary pretrained transformer head defines a conservative energy.
The scoped multi-head construction in Appendix~\ref{app:multihead} uses an
independent tied energy within each head and treats the output projection as
deterministic postprocessing; it is not a claim about a joint Boltzmann law for
arbitrary multi-head attention.

The base model has support around observed memories and should be described as
memory-supported generation. At low $\beta$, novelty and diversity can increase
because samples become noisy. The MNIST experiments lack an external realism
metric, and the protein statistics are computed against the same family used to
construct the representation. A held-out or experimental functional assay is
needed before making a protein-design claim. The submitted VAE used a fixed
latent prior; geometry-aware latent-density baselines remain important. The
former DDPM comparison used an inadequately justified scale and compute
protocol and is not retained as evidence of superiority.

Finite-step ULA has discretization bias, while MALA can mix slowly even when its
acceptance rate is high. Warm-started multi-chain endpoints can reflect the
chosen initialization allocation rather than stationary component weights.
Exact base-target draws should therefore accompany ULA/MALA diagnostics whenever
the GMM identity applies. For constrained targets where it does not, convergence
must instead be assessed with multiple chains, step-size sensitivity, effective
sample size, and application-specific validation.

Several extensions follow naturally: constrained stochastic attention with
smooth biochemical or physical energies, hard-constraint variants using
reflected or proximal dynamics, coupled multi-head energy models, and annealed
or tempered transitions for multimodal targets. These are not repairs to a
defective base method; they are the broader cases for which the sampling
formulation was designed.
