% discussion.tex -- Discussion and Conclusion

The central result of this study is a unified account of deterministic and
stochastic attention on the modern Hopfield energy. In the tied-key/value
model, the attention update supplies the nonlinear term in the exact score, so
Langevin sampling reuses the attention calculation while adding a restoring
drift and calibrated noise. For the unmodified energy, completing the square
also gives an exact Gaussian-mixture representation. Continuous-time Langevin
dynamics, correctly adjusted Metropolis sampling, and independent ancestral
sampling therefore target the same base distribution, while finite-step ULA
approximates it. This identity does not make the stochastic formulation
redundant. Instead, it identifies a tractable special case, supplies an exact
reference for validating the dynamics, and separates the distribution being
sampled from the numerical route used to sample it. The distinction becomes
essential when an application-specific energy is added, because the exact
mixture sampler usually disappears while the gradient-based construction
remains available.

The exact representation also changed how the experimental evidence should be
interpreted. For unit-normalized memories, the base distribution is an
isotropic Parzen estimator with component standard deviation
$\beta^{-1/2}$; with unequal memory norms, the component probabilities require
the norm-dependent weights in Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-mixture}. Exact draws
therefore exposed two misleading comparisons in the original analysis: a
matched Parzen estimator is the same distribution rather than a competing
model, and a Gaussian perturbation using the one-step innovation variance is
too narrow to represent the stationary target. The analytic validation showed
that exact sampling, ULA, and MALA agreed at small step sizes, while the
step-size sweep in Fig.~\ref{fig:stepsize-sweep} showed increasing ULA--MALA
differences and collapsing MALA acceptance at larger steps. Together, these
results support the implementation and the target identity without treating a
high acceptance rate or agreement on a few endpoint summaries as proof of
rapid mixing.

Inverse temperature had two related but distinct effects. It concentrated the
attention responsibilities and simultaneously reduced the Gaussian width
around each selected memory. Responsibility entropy therefore described how a
fixed query allocated mass across a finite memory bank, but its inflection was
not a universal phase boundary or an automatic choice of sampling temperature.
The image spectrum in Fig.~\ref{fig:temp-spectrum-digit8} made the second
effect explicit: decreasing $\beta$ moved endpoints farther from the memories,
but the corresponding increase in novelty and diversity eventually coincided
with the loss of recognizable digit structure. Finite trajectories added a
separate dependence on initialization, integration step, and run length. At
high $\beta$, the tested chains remained local to their warm starts; at lower
$\beta$, nearest-memory labels changed more readily. These observations are
useful for understanding stochastic attention as a transition kernel, but they
do not give a mixing-time result or show that broader samples are better
samples.

The application results were consistent with a memory-supported generator
rather than a model that learned new support. On MNIST, exact and finite-chain
endpoints were local perturbations of stored digits, and the available geometry
metrics did not establish perceptual quality. In the PF00076 experiment, the
memory distribution and PCA decoder reproduced selected in-sample positional
and pairwise statistics more closely than the tested VAE at similar novelty.
The protein diagnostics also showed that the decoded samples retained the
broad positional pattern of the family while shifting toward consensus
residues (Fig.~\ref{fig:protein}), which explains part of the apparent
improvement and limits the biological claim. The experiment did not test
folding, binding, function, or held-out sequences. On Olivetti faces, hard
masking restricted the categorical support to one subject and sharply reduced
leakage to other subjects; it did not learn a new conditional image model. The
financial and multi-head analyses
remain useful as supplementary diagnostics, but their postprocessing and
implementation choices prevent broader claims about either domain.

The broader value of gradient-based sampling lies in targets that are not
covered by the exact mixture theorem. A smooth constraint, observation model,
mechanistic penalty, or design criterion can be added to the Hopfield energy,
and its gradient enters the same stochastic-attention update without requiring
the normalizing constant. This generality comes with a practical rule: exact
base draws should be used when they solve the stated problem, analytically
tractable constraints should be simplified directly, and Langevin dynamics
should be reserved for targets whose added structure prevents independent
sampling or when the trajectory itself is of interest. Each gradient step
costs $\mathcal O(dK)$ plus the cost of the constraint gradient, whereas an
exact base draw requires a component draw and one Gaussian perturbation after
the weights have been computed. Subsequent protein generation,
multiplicity-conditioning, and coagulation studies used this same memory
energy in distinct application settings
\cite{varnerTrainingFreeProtein2026,varnerConditioningProtein2026,varnerSyntheticPatient2026},
illustrating why the analytically tractable base case and the more general
sampling construction should both remain part of the framework.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The exact mixture theorem requires tied keys and values in a common state
space, the quadratic Hopfield state term, and no generic nonlinear energy tilt;
it does not imply that an arbitrary pretrained transformer layer defines the
same scalar energy. The base distribution places isotropic mass around stored
memories and does not learn support beyond that representation. The unadjusted
Langevin algorithm has finite-step discretization bias, MALA can mix slowly
even with high acceptance, and warm-started chains can retain the component allocation imposed at
initialization. The image analyses lacked an external realism measure, and the
flattened-vector diffusion diagnostic was not a competitive test of modern
image diffusion systems. The protein statistics were computed from the same
small family used to construct the memory and decoder, while the finance study
used application-specific correction and did not reproduce volatility
clustering. Most importantly, this paper did not test the constrained
$E+\lambda C$ formulation with a nontrivial state-dependent constraint.
Constrained applications therefore require their own convergence diagnostics,
matched proposal-and-filter baselines, held-out validation, and
application-specific evidence.
