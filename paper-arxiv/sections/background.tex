Let $\mathbf{X}=[\mathbf{m}_1,\ldots,\mathbf{m}_K]\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times K}$
store $K$ memory vectors. The tied-key/value modern Hopfield retrieval map is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{T}_{\beta,\mathbf r}(\boldsymbol{\xi})
=\mathbf{X}\,\operatorname{softmax}\!\left(
\beta\mathbf{X}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\xi}+\log\mathbf r\right),
\label{eq:hopfield-update}
\end{equation}
where $\beta>0$ and $\mathbf r\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq0}^{K}$ contains optional
component multiplicities. A zero entry is interpreted as a hard mask. With
equal multiplicities this is the continuous modern Hopfield update of Ramsauer
et al.~\cite{ramsauerHopfieldNetworksAll2021}. It also has the algebraic form of
single-query dot-product attention when keys and values are tied. This tied
construction is narrower than a general transformer head, which may have
different key and value vectors and an output projection.

Ignoring additive constants, the associated energy is
\begin{equation}
E_{\beta,\mathbf r}(\boldsymbol{\xi})
=\frac12\lVert\boldsymbol{\xi}\rVert_2^2
-\frac1\beta\log\sum_{k=1}^K
r_k\exp\!\left(\beta\mathbf m_k^\top\boldsymbol{\xi}\right).
\label{eq:modern-hopfield-energy}
\end{equation}
Its gradient is
\begin{equation}
\nabla E_{\beta,\mathbf r}(\boldsymbol{\xi})
=\boldsymbol{\xi}-\mathbf{T}_{\beta,\mathbf r}(\boldsymbol{\xi}).
\label{eq:energy-gradient}
\end{equation}
Thus the gradient is the identity minus the attention map; it is not the
attention map itself. Deterministic retrieval applies the map in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:hopfield-update}, or equivalently takes a unit gradient-descent
step on Eq.~\eqref{eq:modern-hopfield-energy}.

Given a target $p(\mathbf x)\propto\exp[-U(\mathbf x)]$, overdamped Langevin
dynamics follows
\begin{equation}
d\mathbf x_t=-\nabla U(\mathbf x_t)\,dt+\sqrt{2}\,d\mathbf B_t,
\end{equation}
and ULA discretizes this process as
\begin{equation}
\mathbf x_{t+1}=\mathbf x_t-\eta\nabla U(\mathbf x_t)
+\sqrt{2\eta}\,\boldsymbol\epsilon_t,
\qquad \boldsymbol\epsilon_t\sim\mathcal N(\mathbf0,\mathbf I).
\label{eq:ula}
\end{equation}
ULA is generally biased at finite step size; MALA corrects its
state-dependent Gaussian proposal with a Metropolis--Hastings ratio
\cite{robertsTweedieExponentialConvergence1996,durmusNonasymptoticAnalysisUnadjusted2017}.
Neither dynamics is necessary when the target itself admits a cheap independent
sampler. We derive that sampler next.
