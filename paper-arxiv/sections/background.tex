\subsection{Attention, Hopfield energy, and Langevin dynamics}

The construction requires one specific connection between attention and
associative memory. A transformer computes scaled dot-product attention from
queries $\mathbf Q$, keys $\mathbf K$, and values $\mathbf V$ as follows:
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Attention}(\mathbf Q,\mathbf K,\mathbf V)
=\operatorname{softmax}\!\left(
\frac{\mathbf Q\mathbf K^\top}{\sqrt d}\right)\mathbf V.
\label{eq:sdp-attention}
\end{equation}
The softmax is applied row-wise, so each output row is a deterministic convex
combination of value vectors. We focus on the tied-key/value case in which the
state, keys, and values share a common space; a general transformer may instead
use distinct projections, residual streams, and output maps. The
associative-memory interpretation begins with the classical Hopfield network
\cite{hopfieldNeuralNetworksPhysical1982}. For binary states
$\mathbf s\in\{-1,+1\}^d$, its quadratic energy is:
\begin{equation}
E_{\mathrm{classical}}(\mathbf s)
=-\tfrac12\mathbf s^\top\mathbf W\mathbf s-\mathbf b^\top\mathbf s,
\qquad
\mathbf W=\frac1d\sum_{k=1}^K\mathbf m_k\mathbf m_k^\top.
\label{eq:classical-hopfield}
\end{equation}
Coordinate-wise sign updates descend this energy, but its storage capacity is
linear in the state dimension \cite{amitStoringInfiniteNumbers1985}. Modern
Hopfield networks replace the quadratic memory interaction with a log-sum-exp
term \cite{ramsauerHopfieldNetworksAll2021}, giving the continuous energy used
below.

Let $\mathbf X=[\mathbf m_1,\ldots,\mathbf m_K]\in\mathbb R^{d\times K}$
store the memory vectors, let $\beta>0$ be the inverse temperature, and let
$\mathbf r\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^K$ contain optional component multiplicities
with $R=\sum_k r_k>0$. We interpret $\log 0=-\infty$, so a zero multiplicity
acts as a hard mask. The retrieval map, associated modern Hopfield energy, and
its gradient are:
\begin{align}
\mathbf T_{\beta,\mathbf r}(\boldsymbol\xi)
&=\mathbf X\operatorname{softmax}\!\left(
\beta\mathbf X^\top\boldsymbol\xi+\log\mathbf r\right),
\label{eq:hopfield-update}\\
E_{\beta,\mathbf r}(\boldsymbol\xi)
&=\frac12\lVert\boldsymbol\xi\rVert_2^2
-\frac1\beta\log\sum_{k=1}^K
r_k\exp\!\left(\beta\mathbf m_k^\top\boldsymbol\xi\right),
\label{eq:modern-hopfield-energy}\\
\nabla E_{\beta,\mathbf r}(\boldsymbol\xi)
&=\boldsymbol\xi-\mathbf T_{\beta,\mathbf r}(\boldsymbol\xi).
\label{eq:energy-gradient}
\end{align}
Equal multiplicities recover the standard continuous modern Hopfield update.
For a single query with tied keys and values, the first line has the same
algebraic form as dot-product attention at the corresponding temperature. The
last line is the key identity for sampling: the attention output supplies the
nontrivial part of the energy gradient, while the state supplies the identity
term. The energy is also confining. If
$M=\max_k\lVert\mathbf m_k\rVert_2$, the log-sum-exp inequality gives the
following lower bound:
\begin{equation}
E_{\beta,\mathbf r}(\boldsymbol\xi)
\geq \frac12\lVert\boldsymbol\xi\rVert_2^2
-M\lVert\boldsymbol\xi\rVert_2-\frac1\beta\log R.
\label{eq:energy-lower-bound}
\end{equation}
The quadratic term dominates as the state norm grows, so the corresponding
Boltzmann density is normalizable. Proposition~\ref{prop:regularity} states the
regularity and dissipativity bounds used later, and
Appendix~\ref{app:proof-regularity} gives the proof.

The Hopfield energy can therefore serve directly as a sampling target. For a
differentiable, normalizable density proportional to the exponential of a
negative potential, overdamped Langevin dynamics and its unadjusted discrete
approximation are:
\begin{align}
d\mathbf x_t
&=-\nabla U(\mathbf x_t)\,dt+\sqrt2\,d\mathbf B_t,
\label{eq:langevin-sde}\\
\mathbf x_{t+1}
&=\mathbf x_t-\eta\nabla U(\mathbf x_t)
+\sqrt{2\eta}\,\boldsymbol\epsilon_t,
\qquad
\boldsymbol\epsilon_t\sim\mathcal N(\mathbf0,\mathbf I).
\label{eq:ula}
\end{align}
Here $\mathbf B_t$ is standard Brownian motion and $\eta>0$ is the integration
step. Under standard regularity and recurrence conditions, the continuous
process has the desired stationary density
\cite{robertsTweedieExponentialConvergence1996}. The finite-step unadjusted
chain is generally biased, although its error vanishes with the step size under
suitable assumptions \cite{durmusNonasymptoticAnalysisUnadjusted2017}. A
Metropolis accept-reject step removes this discretization bias. Together, the
attention-gradient identity and Langevin construction define stochastic
attention: the general method samples from a Hopfield energy modified by an
optional application-specific term, while the unmodified Hopfield target is an
important special case that also admits exact sampling from a Gaussian mixture.
