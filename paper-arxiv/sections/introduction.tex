Attention and modern Hopfield retrieval share a familiar computation: a query
is mapped to a softmax-weighted average of stored vectors
\cite{vaswaniAttentionAllYou2017,ramsauerHopfieldNetworksAll2021}. Stochastic
attention turns this deterministic update into an energy-based sampler by
adding the drift and noise prescribed by Langevin dynamics. The result uses the
same primitives as attention, requires no learned score network, and produces a
trajectory whose target is defined directly by the memory energy.
The construction has since been used for low-data protein sequence generation,
pattern-multiplicity conditioning, and synthetic coagulation trajectories
\cite{varnerTrainingFreeProtein2026,varnerConditioningProtein2026,
varnerSyntheticPatient2026}.

This paper is intended as the theoretical basis for that construction. The
central object is not one particular endpoint-sampling trick, but a general
recipe: if attention supplies the gradient of an energy, Langevin dynamics can
sample from the corresponding Boltzmann law. Moreover, an application-specific
energy can be added to form
\begin{equation}
p_{\beta,\lambda}(\boldsymbol\xi)\propto
\exp\{-\beta[E(\boldsymbol\xi)+\lambda C(\boldsymbol\xi)]\}.
\end{equation}
The additional term may encode an observation, soft physical constraint,
fitness objective, or another energy expert. This is the regime in which the
sampling formulation matters most: the composite target generally has no
closed-form normalizer or direct ancestral sampler, but its gradient remains
available.

The tied-key/value modern Hopfield energy also has a useful special structure.
Completing the square shows that its unmodified Boltzmann law is a finite
isotropic Gaussian mixture centered on the stored memories. Softmax attention
gives the posterior component responsibilities, and attention minus state is
the exact mixture score. Consequently, the base model admits two complementary
sampling routes: stochastic attention via Langevin dynamics and exact
ancestral mixture sampling. They target the same distribution (up to the
finite-step discretization error of ULA), so the exact sampler provides a rare
ground-truth calibration for the more general energy-based method.

This perspective strengthens rather than removes the original construction.
The Gaussian-mixture identity is restricted to the unmodified tied
key/value energy with its quadratic state term. Generic constraints, likelihood
factors, coupled experts, and other energy modifications destroy the finite
mixture form while leaving ULA or MALA applicable. Even in the base case,
finite-time trajectories, warm-started conditional exploration, and sequential
sampling are meaningful algorithmic objects distinct from independent
equilibrium endpoints.

We make five contributions. First, we derive stochastic attention from
Langevin dynamics and state the constrained-energy generalization explicitly.
Second, we give the ULA and MALA algorithms together with regularity and
convergence results for the Hopfield potential. Third, we derive the exact
Gaussian-mixture law for the base tied model, identify attention with posterior
responsibility, and use exact draws to validate the samplers. Fourth, we retain
the temperature, masking, finite-time trajectory, and scoped multi-head theory
that downstream applications build upon. Fifth, we reanalyze the MNIST and
protein experiments to separate properties of the target distribution from
finite-time sampling effects and to state clearly what the reported geometric
diagnostics do and do not establish.
