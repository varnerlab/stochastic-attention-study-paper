\section{Normalization and Score Derivation}
\label{app:normalization}

For completeness, the common normalization in
Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-mixture} is
\begin{equation}
Z_{\beta,\mathbf r}
=\left(\frac{2\pi}{\beta}\right)^{d/2}
\sum_{k=1}^K r_k
\exp\!\left(\frac\beta2\lVert\mathbf m_k\rVert_2^2\right).
\end{equation}
With
$w_k\propto r_k\exp(\beta\lVert\mathbf m_k\rVert_2^2/2)$, the log density is
\begin{equation}
\log p(\boldsymbol\xi)
=\log\sum_k w_k
\left(\frac\beta{2\pi}\right)^{d/2}
\exp\!\left[-\frac\beta2
\lVert\boldsymbol\xi-\mathbf m_k\rVert_2^2\right].
\end{equation}
Differentiation yields
\begin{align}
\nabla\log p(\boldsymbol\xi)
&=\sum_k \Pr(Z=k\mid\boldsymbol\xi)
[-\beta(\boldsymbol\xi-\mathbf m_k)]\\
&=\beta\left[\sum_k a_k(\boldsymbol\xi)\mathbf m_k
-\boldsymbol\xi\right],
\end{align}
which is Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact-score}. The posterior responsibility simplifies to
$a_k\propto r_k\exp(\beta\mathbf m_k^\top\boldsymbol\xi)$ because the norm
factor in $w_k$ cancels the negative component-norm term from the Gaussian
quadratic.

\section{MALA Correction}
\label{app:mala}

For proposal variance $\sigma_q^2=2\alpha/\beta$, define
\begin{equation}
q(\mathbf y\mid\mathbf x)
=\mathcal N(\mathbf y;\boldsymbol\mu(\mathbf x),
\sigma_q^2\mathbf I).
\end{equation}
For the constrained target, let
$\ell(\mathbf x)=-\beta[E_{\beta,\mathbf r}(\mathbf x)+\lambda C(\mathbf x)]$
denote its unnormalized log density and define
$\boldsymbol\mu(\mathbf x)=
\mathbf x+(\alpha/\beta)\nabla\ell(\mathbf x)$. The complete adjusted sampler
is given in Algorithm~\ref{alg:mala}.

\begin{algorithm}[ht]
\caption{MALA Stochastic Attention Sampler}
\label{alg:mala}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Initial state $\boldsymbol\xi_0$, log target $\ell$, inverse
temperature $\beta>0$, step size $\alpha>0$, iterations $T$
\FOR{$t=0,\ldots,T-1$}
\STATE $\boldsymbol\mu_t\leftarrow\boldsymbol\xi_t
+(\alpha/\beta)\nabla\ell(\boldsymbol\xi_t)$
\STATE $\boldsymbol\epsilon_t\sim\mathcal N(\mathbf0,\mathbf I)$
\STATE $\boldsymbol\xi^\star\leftarrow\boldsymbol\mu_t
+\sqrt{2\alpha/\beta}\,\boldsymbol\epsilon_t$
\STATE $\boldsymbol\mu^\star\leftarrow\boldsymbol\xi^\star
+(\alpha/\beta)\nabla\ell(\boldsymbol\xi^\star)$
\STATE $\log R\leftarrow\ell(\boldsymbol\xi^\star)-\ell(\boldsymbol\xi_t)
-\frac{\beta}{4\alpha}\left(
\lVert\boldsymbol\xi_t-\boldsymbol\mu^\star\rVert_2^2
-\lVert\boldsymbol\xi^\star-\boldsymbol\mu_t\rVert_2^2\right)$
\STATE $u_t\sim\operatorname{Uniform}(0,1)$
\IF{$\log u_t<\min(0,\log R)$}
\STATE $\boldsymbol\xi_{t+1}\leftarrow\boldsymbol\xi^\star$
\ELSE
\STATE $\boldsymbol\xi_{t+1}\leftarrow\boldsymbol\xi_t$
\ENDIF
\ENDFOR
\RETURN $\boldsymbol\xi_0,\ldots,\boldsymbol\xi_T$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The log acceptance ratio is
\begin{align}
\log R
={}&\log p(\boldsymbol\xi')-\log p(\boldsymbol\xi)\\
&-\frac{\beta}{4\alpha}
\left[\lVert\boldsymbol\xi-\boldsymbol\mu(\boldsymbol\xi')\rVert_2^2
-\lVert\boldsymbol\xi'-\boldsymbol\mu(\boldsymbol\xi)\rVert_2^2\right].
\end{align}
The second line cannot generally be dropped. For the base target, the
implementation evaluates the normalized mixture log density directly and is
tested against finite-difference scores. For a constrained target, its
normalizing constant cancels in the acceptance ratio, so only the unnormalized
energy and gradient are required.

\section{Analytic Target Validation Details}
\label{app:analytic-validation}

The validation memory matrix is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf X=
\begin{bmatrix}
-1.00 & 0.20 & 1.35\\
 0.00 & 1.10 & -0.25
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad \beta=1.5.
\end{equation}
The unequal norms give analytic component weights
$(0.2411,0.2908,0.4682)$. If
$\boldsymbol\mu=\sum_k w_k\mathbf m_k$, then
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Cov}(\boldsymbol\xi)
=\beta^{-1}\mathbf I+
\sum_k w_k(\mathbf m_k-\boldsymbol\mu)
(\mathbf m_k-\boldsymbol\mu)^\top.
\end{equation}
The exact sampler used 100,000 independent draws. ULA and MALA each used 16
chains of 100,000 steps, 20,000 burn-in steps, and thinning by 20. Chains were
initialized from exact target draws so the experiment measures finite-step
behavior rather than an intentionally poor initialization.

\section{MNIST Protocol and Local Baselines}
\label{app:mnist-protocol}

We used the first 100 images returned by the repository's fixed MNIST loader for
digit 3, transposed and flattened each image to 784 dimensions, and normalized
each memory to unit $\ell_2$ norm. The submitted ULA protocol used 30 memories
sampled without replacement with seed 42, one chain per selected memory,
$T=5{,}000$, burn-in 2,000, thinning by 100, and five retained endpoints per
chain. The exact all-component experiment drew 150 independent endpoints from
all 100 components. Its uncertainty summary repeats the complete 150-endpoint
experiment with 20 seeds and reports mean $\pm$ standard deviation across
repetitions.

The initialization-matched exact diagnostic fixes five draws at each of the 30
submitted warm-start components. It more closely mimics the chain allocation
but is not an ancestral draw from the stationary all-component law. At
$\beta=2000$, it gives novelty 0.152 and diversity 0.601; at $\beta=200$, it
gives novelty 0.550 and diversity 0.888.

\paragraph{$k$NN perturbation.}
For each endpoint, we select a random memory, choose one of its $k$ nearest
cosine neighbors, draw a uniform interpolation coefficient, and add isotropic
noise. The main table uses $k=5$ and $\sigma=\beta^{-1/2}$ at $\beta=200$.

experiment.

\section{Protein Protocol and Decoder Mechanism}
\label{app:protein-protocol}

We used the cached Pfam PF00076 seed alignment, removed insert columns and
columns with more than 50\% gaps, and retained 68 sequences of length 71.
Sequences were one-hot encoded over 20 amino acids, PCA was fit to 95\% retained
variance, and the resulting 59-dimensional memory vectors were normalized.
Generated vectors were reconstructed through the same PCA model and decoded by
per-position argmax. All PCA fitting, distributional references, and HMM
evaluation use the same 68-sequence family; no held-out split is implied.

The all-component exact sampler chooses among all 68 memories. The
initialization-matched diagnostic fixes five draws at each of the 30 submitted
warm-start components. At $\beta=77$, the matched diagnostic gives AA-KL
0.1071, Pos-KL 5.6574, and MI correlation 0.7414. At $\beta=8$, it gives AA-KL
0.0606, Pos-KL 3.3591, and MI correlation 0.8432. Differences between this
diagnostic and the all-component law quantify some of the sensitivity to
finite sample allocation.

The decoder itself explains part of the low-temperature behavior. For wider
Gaussian components, inverse PCA adds the learned family mean before argmax.
Strongly conserved positions are therefore pulled toward consensus residues,
while variable positions remain sensitive to the sampled perturbation. This is
a property of the representation and decoder, not a distinct effect of ULA.

\section{Responsibility Concentration}
\label{app:responsibility-concentration}

The earlier load-ratio heatmap is best interpreted as a finite-dimensional map
of responsibility concentration
$C=1-H(\mathbf a)/\log K$. Lucibello and M\'ezard analyze related condensation
and retrieval boundaries for random ensembles
\cite{lucibelloExponentialCapacityDense2024}. Our empirical $C=0.5$ contour is
not an exact capacity boundary, and the sufficient Hessian bound
$\beta\lVert\mathbf X\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}^2<2$ is not an exact onset of
nonconvexity. We do not use either quantity to claim automatic temperature
selection in the revised paper.

\section{Experiments Removed from the Evidentiary Claim}
\label{app:removed-experiments}

We do not retain the submitted DDPM comparison as evidence: the unit-normalized
data, forward-noise schedule, and training-compute comparison were not matched
well enough to support a conclusion about diffusion models. The additional
image, protein, finance, and multi-head experiments are retained elsewhere in
the appendix as finite-protocol evidence and scoped demonstrations, but they are
not used to claim superiority over learned generators.

\section{Code and Reproduction}
\label{app:code}

The repository contains the exact density, analytic weights, posterior
responsibilities, score, exact sampler, ULA, and MALA in `code/src/Compute.jl`.
The test suite checks unequal norms, multiplicities, score finite differences,
component frequencies, and reproducibility. The principal commands are:
\begin{verbatim}
cd code
julia --project=. test/runtests.jl
julia --project=. run_exact_target_validation.jl

cd mnist-experiment
julia --project=. run_exact_mixture_baseline.jl

cd ../sequence-experiment
julia --project=. run_exact_mixture_protein.jl
\end{verbatim}
All commands use fixed seeds. The exact MNIST and analytic validation scripts
run without model training. The protein script uses cached Pfam alignment and
HMM files when present and requires HMMER for the HMM diagnostic.
