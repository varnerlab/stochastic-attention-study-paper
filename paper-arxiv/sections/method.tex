\subsection{The exact Boltzmann law}

We begin with the probability model rather than a numerical sampler. Let
$p_{\beta,\mathbf r}(\boldsymbol\xi)$ be the normalized Boltzmann density of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:modern-hopfield-energy}:
\begin{equation}
p_{\beta,\mathbf r}(\boldsymbol\xi)
=Z_{\beta,\mathbf r}^{-1}
\exp[-\beta E_{\beta,\mathbf r}(\boldsymbol\xi)].
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Exact Gaussian-mixture representation]
\label{thm:exact-mixture}
For $\beta>0$ and at least one positive $r_k$,
\begin{equation}
p_{\beta,\mathbf r}(\boldsymbol\xi)
=\sum_{k=1}^K w_k\,
\mathcal N(\boldsymbol\xi;\mathbf m_k,\beta^{-1}\mathbf I),
\qquad
w_k=\frac{r_k\exp(\frac\beta2\lVert\mathbf m_k\rVert_2^2)}
{\sum_{j=1}^K r_j\exp(\frac\beta2\lVert\mathbf m_j\rVert_2^2)}.
\label{eq:exact-mixture}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Expanding the unnormalized Boltzmann density gives
\begin{align}
\exp[-\beta E_{\beta,\mathbf r}(\boldsymbol\xi)]
&=\sum_{k=1}^K r_k
\exp\!\left(-\frac\beta2\lVert\boldsymbol\xi\rVert_2^2
+\beta\mathbf m_k^\top\boldsymbol\xi\right)\\
&=\sum_{k=1}^K r_k
\exp\!\left(\frac\beta2\lVert\mathbf m_k\rVert_2^2\right)
\exp\!\left[-\frac\beta2
\lVert\boldsymbol\xi-\mathbf m_k\rVert_2^2\right].
\end{align}
Each final factor is proportional to a Gaussian with covariance
$\beta^{-1}\mathbf I$. Normalizing the common Gaussian constant and the sum of
component coefficients yields Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact-mixture}.
\end{proof}

If the memories have equal norm, including the $\ell_2$-normalized memories in
our experiments, the norm factors cancel. Uniform multiplicities then give
$w_k=1/K$. This is exactly an isotropic Parzen estimator with bandwidth
$h=\beta^{-1/2}$. If memory norms differ, uniform component selection is
incorrect; the exponential norm factor in Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact-mixture} must be
used.

Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-mixture} gives the preferred independent endpoint
sampler (Algorithm~\ref{alg:exact-sampler}). Component weights can be
precomputed in $\mathcal O(dK)$ time. Each subsequent sample requires a
categorical draw and $d$ Gaussian variates, rather than $T$ attention
evaluations costing $\mathcal O(TdK)$.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Exact modern-Hopfield endpoint sampler}
\label{alg:exact-sampler}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Memories $\mathbf X$, inverse temperature $\beta>0$, multiplicities
$\mathbf r\geq\mathbf0$, number of endpoints $S$
\STATE $w_k\leftarrow
r_k\exp(\frac\beta2\lVert\mathbf m_k\rVert_2^2)$ for $k=1,\ldots,K$
\STATE Normalize $\mathbf w\leftarrow\mathbf w/(\sum_k w_k)$
\FOR{$s=1,\ldots,S$}
\STATE $z_s\sim\operatorname{Categorical}(\mathbf w)$
\STATE $\boldsymbol\epsilon_s\sim\mathcal N(\mathbf0,\mathbf I)$
\STATE $\boldsymbol\xi_s\leftarrow
\mathbf m_{z_s}+\beta^{-1/2}\boldsymbol\epsilon_s$
\ENDFOR
\RETURN $\{\boldsymbol\xi_s\}_{s=1}^{S}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Attention is posterior responsibility}

Let $Z$ denote the Gaussian component. Substituting the weights from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact-mixture} into Bayes' rule cancels the component-norm terms:
\begin{equation}
\Pr(Z=k\mid\boldsymbol\xi)
=\frac{r_k\exp(\beta\mathbf m_k^\top\boldsymbol\xi)}
{\sum_j r_j\exp(\beta\mathbf m_j^\top\boldsymbol\xi)}.
\label{eq:responsibilities}
\end{equation}
The vector of posterior responsibilities is therefore exactly the biased
softmax in Eq.~\eqref{eq:hopfield-update}. Differentiating the log mixture gives
\begin{equation}
\nabla_{\boldsymbol\xi}\log p_{\beta,\mathbf r}(\boldsymbol\xi)
=\beta\left[\mathbf T_{\beta,\mathbf r}(\boldsymbol\xi)
-\boldsymbol\xi\right].
\label{eq:exact-score}
\end{equation}
This establishes the precise relationship: attention is the posterior mean of
the component center, and attention minus the state is the score divided by
$\beta$.

\subsection{ULA and MALA as optional dynamics}

Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact-score} in ULA with $\eta=\alpha/\beta$ gives
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol\xi_{t+1}
=(1-\alpha)\boldsymbol\xi_t
+\alpha\mathbf T_{\beta,\mathbf r}(\boldsymbol\xi_t)
+\sqrt{\frac{2\alpha}{\beta}}\boldsymbol\epsilon_t.
\label{eq:stochastic-attention}
\end{equation}
We retain the name \emph{stochastic attention} for this transition kernel, not
for the underlying probability law. At finite $\alpha$, ULA has discretization
bias. MALA uses the same proposal mean
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol\mu(\boldsymbol\xi)
=(1-\alpha)\boldsymbol\xi
+\alpha\mathbf T_{\beta,\mathbf r}(\boldsymbol\xi)
\end{equation}
and covariance $2\alpha\beta^{-1}\mathbf I$, but accepts a proposal
$\boldsymbol\xi'$ using
\begin{equation}
\min\!\left\{1,
\frac{p(\boldsymbol\xi')q(\boldsymbol\xi\mid\boldsymbol\xi')}
{p(\boldsymbol\xi)q(\boldsymbol\xi'\mid\boldsymbol\xi)}\right\}.
\label{eq:mala-acceptance}
\end{equation}
The proposal-density ratio is required because the Langevin mean depends on
the current state. High acceptance alone does not establish mixing or agreement
with the target. In the unconstrained model, both algorithms are more expensive
than Algorithm~\ref{alg:exact-sampler} for independent endpoints. They remain
useful diagnostics for discretization, warm-start dependence, and finite-time
trajectories.

\subsection{Responsibility concentration and temperature}

For a fixed state, write $e_k=\mathbf m_k^\top\boldsymbol\xi$ and
$a_k\propto r_k\exp(\beta e_k)$. The responsibility entropy
$H=-\sum_k a_k\log a_k$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
\frac{dH}{d\beta}=-\beta\operatorname{Var}_{\mathbf a}(e)
\label{eq:entropy-derivative}
\end{equation}
when $\mathbf r$ and $\boldsymbol\xi$ are held fixed. Entropy therefore
decreases monotonically with inverse temperature for that query. The location
of maximal entropy loss is a descriptive crossover of the responsibility
vector, not a universal generation threshold and not an exact storage-capacity
boundary. Empirical multipliers applied to such a crossover remain manual
hyperparameters.

Temperature also has a direct probabilistic interpretation:
$\beta^{-1/2}$ is the component standard deviation. For unit-norm memories and
an exact draw $\boldsymbol\xi=\mathbf m_k+\boldsymbol\epsilon$, high-dimensional
norm concentration gives the approximation
\begin{equation}
1-\cos(\boldsymbol\xi,\mathbf m_k)
\approx 1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+d/\beta}}.
\label{eq:novelty-approximation}
\end{equation}
This approximation neglects the noise projection along $\mathbf m_k$ and
assumes that the generating component remains the nearest memory. It is not an
exact formula for novelty defined by a maximum over all $K$ memories.

\subsection{Masking and multiplicity}

Setting $r_k=0$ removes component $k$; positive multiplicities reweight
components according to Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact-mixture}. Thus hard memory-subset
conditioning and multiplicity bias preserve the exact sampler. They are useful
controls over a memory-centered model, but they do not by themselves create a
new distributional family that requires Langevin dynamics.
